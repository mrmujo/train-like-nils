    Send to your Garmin

    Through intervals.icu (free). Every session lands in your Garmin calendar with pace targets, and the watch shows it as the day’s workout. The steady test is its own step, so it gets its own lap.

    1. Sign up at intervals.icu. In Settings, connect Garmin Connect and tick Upload planned workouts.
    2. Further down in Settings, under Developer settings, copy your API key.
    3. Pick when week 1 starts, paste the key and send.

    Your key goes straight from this page to intervals.icu and isn’t saved. intervals.icu uploads the coming week to Garmin Connect, so sync your watch to get it. Sending replaces any Train like Nils sessions from today on, so you can change the race date or the percentage and send again. Past sessions and your other workouts aren’t touched.

    Nils’s rules

    • Train five days, rest two. Monday to Friday on, the weekend fully off. Use the free time to have a life outside running. Nils says that is what kept him going.
    • Stick to the Limit. Train close to it, never past it. If your pace at normal effort drops more than about 3 %, stop the session and take two rest days.
    • Test yourself every day. Every warm-up ends with 5 steady minutes, Nils’s 260 W on the bike. Run them at the fast end of your easy pace, at the same pace on the same flat stretch (or a treadmill) every day, and note your heart rate at the end. Over the weeks, a lower heart rate at that pace means you are getting fitter. Within a week it drifts down as you tire, but it should be back after the weekend. Much lower than usual means a tired body: take extra rest. Higher than usual means good shape, or that you are getting sick.
    • Ease into every phase. The first week of each phase is lighter on purpose.
    • Race pace means race pace. On specific days, hit the pace and put the easy running after the fast part, never before.
    • Long days, two sessions. When a day passes 2½ hours, split it with a proper lunch in between.
    • Sick? Rest. Stay off until you are healthy, test with an easy session, and never try to catch up on missed training.
    • Fuel the hard work. Sessions marked Fuel are threshold, race-pace and race days: always carry some sugar, because running out costs you the session and extra recovery. Nils took 100 mg of caffeine 30 min before intervals. Easy hours ran on normal food, with plenty of fat for an even energy flow and a proper lunch between long sessions.
    • Eat for the season. The Aerobic phase is food season: Nils ate about 7000 kcal a day and put on about 5 kg to stay clear of injury. He lost it slowly in the Specific phase, 85 to 80 kg from October to January, eating more on big days and less on small ones and never going short on energy for the hard sessions.
    • Build strength, then let it go. He rebuilt his legs with one-legged squats and some heavy lifting early on, then dropped it and lost about 25 % of his max squat, which didn’t matter for a 10k. Keep five minutes of core and stretching a week as prehab.
    • Keep it simple. Cut anything that steals time from the key sessions.
    • Easy hours can be biked. 1 h of running ≈ 1 h 20 min on the bike, if your legs need a break from the pounding.
    • After the race: Aerobic 2.0. Two weeks of easy volume only before you train hard again.

    Nils wrote his program for himself: an elite adult who already had a big training base. He says most athletes can’t copy it straight away. Scale it, ease in, and talk to a doctor if you have health concerns. 100 % ≈ the 25 h of running a week he said he would have done instead of 33 h on the bike.
